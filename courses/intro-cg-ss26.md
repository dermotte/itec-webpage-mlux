# Introduction to Computer Graphics

This course provides a fundamental understanding of computer graphics and its application in game development. Topics include a mathematics primer, 2D and 3D graphics, shading, and hands-on experience with practical tools.

Grading is based on:

1. Mandatory assignments
2. A final project
3. A final report

## Final Project

The final project is due on **June 25, 2026**. The goal is to apply the concepts learned during the lecture by modeling and rendering a scene in Blender.

### Presentation

On **June 25, 2026**, you will give a 3-minute presentation. Please be prepared to:

1. Show your final result.
2. Outline your specific contributions.
3. Discuss lessons learned and obstacles encountered.

### Submission Checklist

Upload a single ZIP file to Moodle by **June 25, 2026**, containing:

1. Your source files (packed `.blend` file) or a link to a repository (GitHub, Bitbucket, etc.).
2. Your final render (images and video).
3. Your presentation slides.
4. A PDF work report including:
   * A cover page with your name and student number.
   * A body text of approximately 800 words (2 pages) detailing your process and findings.

### Project Requirements

To meet the scope of the project, you must:

* Model at least three objects (e.g., using extrusion).
* Apply materials to the objects (shaders, textures, etc.).
* Populate a scene with your models (and optional imported assets).
* Light the scene effectively with three or more light sources.
* Render both a still image and a short animation.

**Constraints:**

* The project must be created in **Blender**.
* The visual style must be **Low Poly**.
* Use the **Eevee** rendering engine.
* You are not allowed to submit the result from Blender Guru's *donut tutorial*.

## Grading & Course Policies

To receive a passing grade, you must fulfill all requirements precisely. If you encounter issues meeting a deadline, please contact the instructor in advance.

1. **Attendance:** Attend all classes. If you cannot attend, notify the instructor in advance to avoid being removed from the course.
2. **Assignments:** Submit all exercises on time. Late submissions (up to one week) are accepted but will incur a point reduction.
3. **Presentation:** Present your final project in class.
4. **Documentation:** Submit the project files and the accompanying report.

The final grade is calculated using an XP sheet available on Moodle, where you can also track your current progress.

### Report Format

* Follow the [Chicago Manual of Style](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/). You may use this [Overleaf template](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/examples/the-chicago-citation-style-with-biblatex/pdqqrmwtdqpc) or refer to the [sample report](downloads/sample_report_introduction_CG.pdf).
* Ensure consistent formatting throughout the paper.
* Include a bibliography referencing all sources, including videos, websites, and code repositories.
* Demonstrate critical reflection and original observations.

### Academic Integrity

We adhere to the definition of plagiarism provided by the [University of California, Berkeley](https://sa.berkeley.edu/conduct/integrity/definition):

> "Plagiarism is defined as the use of intellectual material produced by another person without acknowledging its source..."

In this course, this includes source code, tutorials, and assets. You must:

* Verify license compatibility (e.g., CC-BY, MIT).
* Properly attribute and cite all external sources in your report.

The [AAU Code of Conduct](https://www.aau.at/en/research/research-profile/good-academic-practice/) applies. Plagiarism or misconduct will result in immediate removal from the course.
