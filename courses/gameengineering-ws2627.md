# Game Engineering

The goal of this course is to introduce students to computer game engineering. Game engineering is not solely a technical discipline; it involves non-technical domains such as creative audio, 3D and visual design, product management and planning, storyboarding and writing, and playtesting.

After completing this course, students will understand the key concepts and roles within a game project, how a game project is set up and executed, and the specific processes involved.

The practical course associated with the lecture takes place as a game jam in December. Further information regarding the game jam will be shared under Practical Game Engineering later in the semester.

## General Course Information

Please note that standard term paper formatting, code of conduct, AI usage, and plagiarism policies apply to all course deliverables where applicable. Failure to adhere to these policies will impact your final grade.

### Paper Format
**Fulfillment of Formal Requirements:**
  - Papers must follow the [Chicago Manual of Style](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/). An [Overleaf LaTeX template](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/examples/the-chicago-citation-style-with-biblatex/pdqqrmwtdqpc) is available.
  - Papers must be consistently formatted and proofread for typos and grammatical errors (e.g., using tools like Grammarly).
  - Papers must include a comprehensive bibliography citing all referenced materials (including online videos, web pages, and code repositories).

**Reliance on Research Literature:**
  - Careful selection of primary and secondary citations.
  - Properly structured and formatted bibliography.

**Academic Analysis:**
  - Effective application of theoretical frameworks.
  - Original observations on the primary source material.
  - Critical reflection on analytical findings.

### AI Policy
While students are encouraged to use available digital tools as study aids or coding reference guides, the objective of the course is for students to develop code and submit original work independently. If AI-generated text or code is suspected in a submission, you may be called to an oral review, which can affect your final grade. Plagiarism guidelines apply to AI-generated content (with the AI model categorized as "another author").

### Code of Conduct and Plagiarism Policies

The [University of California, Berkeley](https://sa.berkeley.edu/conduct/integrity/definition) defines plagiarism as follows:

> Plagiarism is defined as use of intellectual material produced by another person without acknowledging its source, for example:
> * Wholesale copying of passages from works of others into your homework, essay, term paper, or dissertation without acknowledgment.
> * Use of the views, opinions, or insights of another without acknowledgment.
> * Paraphrasing of another person’s characteristic or original phraseology, metaphor, or other literary device without acknowledgment.

For computer science courses, this policy extends to source code, video tutorials, and online repositories used to construct project software. Students are required to verify license compatibility (usage permissions, required attributions, and license constraints) and cite all sources within project documentation. Failure to cite external sources constitutes academic dishonesty.

Please note that the [AAU Code of Conduct](https://www.aau.at/en/research/research-profile/good-academic-practice/) and GSE departmental policies apply. Students committing academic dishonesty or violating the code of conduct will be removed from the course immediately.
