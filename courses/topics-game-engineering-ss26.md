# Advanced Topics in Game Engineering

Throughout this semester, students will work in teams to produce a complete game. Each student will take on a specific role within their team, mirroring a professional studio environment. The process covers the entire development lifecycle: planning, design, development, testing, marketing, and roll-out.

The course is a hybrid of workshops, stand-up meetings, self-organized development sessions, student presentations, and lectures. Guest lectures may be held online (via Twitch.tv) or as inverted classrooms. The ultimate goal is to prepare a high-quality submission for the **PGDA Young Talent Award** (see [Talent Award 2025](https://pgda.at/talent-award-2025/) for more information).

## Deliverables

This course focuses on both the final product (the game) and the process of collaborative development. Your grade is based on your presentations and deliverables, which must document both technical progress and lessons learned.

Documentation requirements vary by role, but every submission must include a reflection on individual growth and potential process improvements.

### Role-Specific Documentation

| Role                 | Document                              | Scope                                                        |
|:---------------------|:--------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Game Designer** | Game Design Document (GDD)            | Game mechanics, level design, systems, etc.                 |
| **Software Developer**| Software Design Document (SDD)        | Technical architecture, requirements, code structure, etc.   |
| **Visual Artist** | Concept Art & Art Design              | Visual identity, asset evolution, and style guides.          |
| **Music / SFX Artist**| Sound & Music Design                 | Audio landscape, SFX implementation, and composition.       |
| **Writer** | Narrative Design                      | Story arcs, dialogue, character bios, and world-building.   |
| **Playtest Engineer**| Playtesting Documentation            | Testing plans, methodology, and analysis of results.        |
| **Producer** | Project Plan & Implementation         | Meeting minutes, scheduling, communication, and milestones. |
| **PR / Community** | Marketing & Documentation             | Campaign plans, execution, and engagement metrics (TikTok, Twitch). |

### Individual Final Report

Each student must submit an individual report of **4,000 words**, structured as follows:

1. **Role-Specific Documentation (3,000 words):** Detailed technical/creative documentation as assigned to your role.
2. **Reflection (1,000 words):**
    * **Victories:** What went well? Describe key moments and achievements you are proud of.
    * **Challenges:** What went wrong? Analyze mistakes, technical hurdles, or interpersonal issues within the group.
    * **Conclusion:** Provide three "take-home messages." How has this experience impacted your professional development? What would you do differently in your next project?

## Grading & Course Policies

To receive a passing grade, you must fulfill the following requirements to the letter. If you anticipate missing a deadline or requirement, please contact the instructor in advance.

1. **Attendance:** Regular participation in all sessions is mandatory.
2. **Project:** Active participation in the creation and presentation of the final project.
3. **Submission:** On-time delivery of all role-specific and individual deliverables.

The final grade is derived from the quality of your presentations and the depth of your final report.

### Paper Format

* Reports must follow the [Chicago Manual of Style](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/). You may use this [Overleaf template](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/examples/the-chicago-citation-style-with-biblatex/pdqqrmwtdqpc) for a strict implementation.
* Formatting must be coherent throughout the document.
* Include a complete bibliography for all sources (videos, web pages, code repositories, etc.).
* Balance theoretical resources with self-made observations and critical reflection.

### Code of Conduct and Plagiarism Policies

We adhere to the [UC Berkeley definition of plagiarism](https://sa.berkeley.edu/conduct/integrity/definition):

> "Plagiarism is defined as the use of intellectual material produced by another person without acknowledging its source..."

In a game engineering context, this includes **source code, video tutorials, and third-party assets**. You are required to:

1. Verify license compatibility for all external assets.
2. Provide proper attribution according to the specific license.
3. Cite all sources in your final report.

The [AAU Code of Conduct](https://www.aau.at/en/research/research-profile/good-academic-practice/) and the GSE Code of Conduct apply. Any violation will result in immediate removal from the course.
